\chapter{Absolute probability versus relational advantage}
\label{ch:absolute_pr}

\ssprove{} measures security relationally. Its headline quantity is the
\emph{advantage}, the difference between two games' success probabilities, and
its whole indistinguishability calculus bounds only such differences. The output
channel asks a different question: the standalone probability that one game lets
a predictor name Bob's secret $V_2$. That standalone quantity is an
\emph{absolute} probability, ``absolute'' marking the contrast with the relative
quantity the relational calculus returns. This chapter fixes the two notions,
records why the relational machinery cannot deliver the absolute one, and states
the two-leg composition that the contrast forces on Part~II.

\begin{definition}[Relational advantage]
  \label{def:relational_advantage}
  \rocqok
  % library: SSProve AdvantageE (Crypt.pkg_advantage); the difference bounded by
  %   eq_rel_perf_ind and the perfect-indistinguishability lemmas.  No \rocq link
  %   (library fact, outside the project's coqdoc), as for lem:exist_pr_code.
  The advantage of a distinguisher $A$ between two closed games is the absolute
  difference of the probabilities that $A$, composed with each game, returns
  true. \ssprove{}'s indistinguishability calculus bounds this difference and
  never the magnitude of either term alone.
  \[ \AdvE(G_0, G_1, A)
        = \bigl|\,\Pr[\,A\circ G_0 = \mathrm{true}\,]
                - \Pr[\,A\circ G_1 = \mathrm{true}\,]\,\bigr| \]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Absolute success probability]
  \label{def:absolute_pr}
  \rocqok
  % library: SSProve Pr / Pr_code / Pr_fst (Crypt.nominal.Pr); the standalone
  %   true-mass of one closed game run from the empty heap.  Reflected as an
  %   iterated sum over the game's samples by lem:exist_pr_code.
  The absolute success probability of one closed game is the true-mass of its
  denotation run from the empty heap. It is a property of a single game, not a
  difference of two.
  \[ \Pr[\,G = \mathrm{true}\,]
        = \mu\bigl(\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{code}}(G)(\emptyset)\bigr)(\mathrm{true}) \]
\end{definition}

\textbf{The obstruction.} Every relational rule rewrites or bounds a difference
$\AdvE(G_0, G_1, A)$; none yields the absolute $\Pr[\,G = \mathrm{true}\,]$ of a
lone game. The bound the fiber argument supplies is exactly such an absolute
statement, the success probability of the single guessing game
(Definition~\ref{def:guessing_experiment_leak_S}),
\[ \Prexp(\zero_S) \le 1/m, \]
so it lies outside the reach of the advantage calculus. The absolute leg is
therefore discharged through the denotational layer of
Definition~\ref{def:absolute_pr} directly: the connector
(Lemma~\ref{lem:guess_success_sdistr_eq_fdist}) reflects $\Prexp(\zero_S)$ as a
distribution probability, and the footprint frame
(Lemma~\ref{lem:Pr_fst_agree_locs}) carries the Infotheo $1/m$ across, never
forming an advantage.

\textbf{The two legs and their join.} The output channel splits into one
absolute leg and one relational leg, joined by the triangle inequality. The
absolute leg bounds the all-zero endpoint's standalone probability by $1/m$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:guess_sdistr_success_le_invm}); the relational leg keeps
the real-to-zero distance inside the advantage world at $2\,\epscpa$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:guess_advantage_le}). One term is a single-game probability
and the other a difference of two, which is why the triangle inequality is what
joins them (Theorem~\ref{thm:dsdp_alice_secrecy_leak_S}):
\[ \underbrace{\Prexp(\real_S)}_{\text{absolute}}
   \;\le\;
   \underbrace{\Prexp(\zero_S)}_{\le\, 1/m\ \text{(absolute leg)}}
   \;+\;
   \underbrace{\bigl|\,\Prexp(\real_S) - \Prexp(\zero_S)\,\bigr|}
             _{\le\, 2\,\epscpa\ \text{(relational leg)}}
   \;\le\; 1/m + 2\,\epscpa. \]

\chapter{Extending the derivation to Alice's decryption}
\label{ch:deriving_output}

In the protocol Alice receives the masked aggregate, decrypts it, and removes
her masks to read the scalar-product output $S=U_1V_1+U_2V_2+U_3V_3$. The Part~I
walk stops one reception short of this decrypt
(Definition~\ref{def:walk_obs}), so the derived view exposes only the
ciphertexts. This chapter continues the same walk across that decrypt-reception,
lowers and denotes the new observation, and so the derived endpoint game exposes
$S$ alongside the ciphertexts. Adjoining $S$ to both endpoints is a common
deterministic post-processing: it adds no encryption hop, so the Part~I bound
$2\,\epscpa$ carries over unchanged.

\end{definition}

\chapter{The output-secrecy guessing experiment}
\label{ch:guessing_experiment}

The output channel is measured not by an advantage but by the absolute
probability that a predictor, reading the view including $S$, names Bob's secret
$V_2$. This chapter builds that guessing experiment on the derived endpoint game
and rewrites its success probability as an Infotheo distribution probability,
the single identity that carries the fiber bound of Chapter~\ref{ch:it_bound} to
the \ssprove{} side. The identity is read off the experiment as a pushforward:
the distribution of the $(\guess,V_2)$ pair the experiment produces is itself an
Infotheo distribution, and the success probability is the probability of its
diagonal.

\begin{definition}[Guessing experiment on the endpoint game]
  \label{def:guessing_experiment_leak_S}
  \rocq{infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.predictor_guesser,
       infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guessing_challenger,
       infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guessing_experiment}
  \rocqok
  \uses{def:games_leak_s,def:absolute_pr}
  A predictor reads the leaked ciphertexts and the output $S$ and returns a
  guess. The experiment runs it against the endpoint game and tests
  $\guess = V_2$. Its success probability is written $\Prexp(\text{game})$.
  \[ \Prexp(\text{game})
        = \mu\big(\mathrm{Pr}(\,\text{challenger}\circ
        (\text{predictor}\parallel\text{game})\,)\big)(\mathrm{true}) \]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[\ssprove{} absolute-$\mathrm{Pr}$ reflection calculus (library)]
  \label{lem:exist_pr_code}
  \rocqok
  % library: Pr_code_ret/_sample/_bind/_get/_put, Pr_fst_sample/_bind, Pr_Pr_fst
  %   (Crypt.nominal.Pr), dlet_uniform (Crypt.HybridArgument), distr.dmargin*.
  SSProve's nominal layer reflects a closed program's probability as an iterated
  sum over its sample operations, a uniform draw of domain $n$ contributing the
  factor $1/n$, a cell read returning the value last written, and a bind
  reflecting compositionally even through an opaque continuation. Its
  subdistribution type is the mathcomp-analysis distribution
  $\{\mathrm{distr}\ A/R\}$.
  \[ \mathrm{Pr}(x \leftarrow \mathrm{sample}\ \mathrm{unif}\,n \,;;\, k\,x)
        = \tfrac1n{\textstyle\sum_x}\mathrm{Pr}(k\,x) \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[The guessing-experiment joint distribution]
  \label{def:guess_joint_fdist}
  \rocq{infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guess_joint_fdist,
       infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guess_joint_code}
  \rocqok
  % renamed from dsdp_guess_fdist / dsdp_guess_core (prefix policy drops dsdp_
  %   on section-locals; guess_joint_* carries the (guess, V2) role).
  The Infotheo fdist of the $(\guess,V_2)$ pair the experiment produces: it is
  the pushforward $\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{fst}}$ of the closed pair-returning code
  (the experiment without the equality test) into the finite carrier.
  \[ \jointfdist : \{\mathrm{fdist}\ (\guess,V_2)\}
        = \mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{fst}}(\text{pair code}) \]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The game probability is the joint diagonal]
  \label{lem:guess_success_sdistr_eq_fdist}
  \rocq{infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guess_sdistr_success,
       infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guess_fdist_success,
       infotheo.dumas2017dual.dsdp.dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber.guess_success_sdistr_eq_fdist}
  \rocqok
  % renamed from Pr_guess_enc_zero_leak_S_eqE, restructured to name both sides:
  %   guess_sdistr_success (SSProve subdistribution mass) = guess_fdist_success
  %   (Infotheo fdist diagonal mass), mirroring sdistr_to_fdist.
  \uses{def:games_leak_s,def:guess_joint_fdist,def:guessing_experiment_leak_S,lem:exist_pr_code}
  % proof (committed): the bool experiment is the pair code post-composed with
  %   the equality test; Pr_Pr_fst + the bind law (Pr_code_bind) factor the test
  %   through the pair code's distribution, which IS the joint fdist (predictor
  %   opaque throughout).  Carries one hypothesis: the predictor is lossless.
  $\Prexp(\zero_S)$ equals the probability, under the joint fdist
  (Definition~\ref{def:guess_joint_fdist}), of the diagonal event
  $\guess = V_2$. The bool experiment is the pair code followed by the equality
  test, so its success probability is the diagonal mass of the pair code's
  distribution; the predictor is never inspected.
  \[ \Prexp(\zero_S) = \Pr\nolimits_{\jointfdist}[\,\guess = V_2\,] \]
\end{lemma}

\chapter{The information-theoretic bound and full secrecy}
\label{ch:it_bound}

Conditioned on $S$ and her inputs, Bob's secret $V_2$ is uniform over a fiber of
$m$ points, so any guess succeeds with probability at most $1/m$. Infotheo
proves this over an abstract joint distribution. This chapter reflects the
experiment's own samples into an explicit such distribution, frames the opaque
predictor out of the secret cell so the guess depends on $V_2$ only through $S$,
discharges Infotheo's hypotheses, and pushes the resulting $1/m$ through the
connector and the computational distance to the full bound $1/m + 2\,\epscpa$.

  \[ \#\lvert \fib(u_1,u_2,u_3,v_1,s)\rvert = m \]

\begin{lemma}[Conditional uniformity on the fiber]

  \[ \Pr[\,(V_2,V_3)=(v_2,v_3)\mid \mathrm{Cond}=\cond\,] = m^{-1} \]
\end{lemma}

\section{Reflecting the experiment and framing the predictor}

\begin{lemma}[Footprint of valid code]
  \label{lem:Pr_fst_agree_locs}
  \uses{lem:exist_pr_code}
  % to create: Pr_fst_agree_locs (dsdp_security_indcpa_fiber), with
  %   Pr_fst_closed the L = emptym corollary.  Proof: induction on valid_code;
  %   the get/put cases touch only L, the op case is vacuous for the empty
  %   import interface, sample/ret thread the heap.
  The value-marginal of valid code depends only on the heap restricted to the
  code's own locations $L$: heaps agreeing on $L$ give equal
  $\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{fst}}$. This is the separation-logic footprint property
  --- a program cannot observe state outside the locations it reads or writes
  --- and it is what makes the predictor, whose $L$ excludes the secret cell,
  blind to $V_2$.
  \[ h =_{L} h' \;\Longrightarrow\;
        \mathrm{dmargin}_{\mathrm{fst}}(\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{code}}\,c\,h)
        = \mathrm{dmargin}_{\mathrm{fst}}(\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{code}}\,c\,h') \]
\end{lemma}

  \label{def:guess_sample_fdist}

\end{definition}

\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Full secrecy by composition]
