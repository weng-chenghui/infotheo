# PGG-SMC Pivot Analysis: Alternative Framings for ProvSec

**Date:** 2026-03-21
**Method:** 6-agent explore-audit (3 research + 3 audit)
**Starting premise:** PGG-SMC loses to Shamir as a secret sharing scheme on every metric -- framing it as one invites unfavorable comparison and obscures the real contributions.

---

## Executive Summary

Three alternative framings for PGG-SMC were investigated: Barrington's theorem / oblivious branching programs, oblivious permutation evaluation, and mixing networks. **All three failed audit.** The structural similarities are real but superficial -- different operations wearing similar algebra. PGG-SMC sits in a "dead zone": theoretically interesting but practically isolated, with no established crypto primitive it naturally instantiates.

---

## Direction 1: Barrington's Theorem / Oblivious Branching Programs

### Research Findings

- PGG-SMC's `word_eval` computes a product of permutations from generators, structurally identical to branching program evaluation. The codebase has a direct S5 instance with adjacent transpositions.
- Barrington IS used in modern cryptography: indistinguishability obfuscation (GGH+RSW FOCS 2013), randomized encodings (Ishai-Kushilevitz 2002), group-based FHE (Nuida 2014, GRAFHEN 2025), Commuting Permutation Systems for MPC (Agarwal-Anand-Prabhakaran EUROCRYPT 2019).
- No Barrington formalization exists in any proof assistant (confirmed via web search).
- **Key gap:** Barrington selects permutations based on INPUT BITS (computation); PGG selects from a RANDOM WORD (secret sharing). No circuit-to-word compilation in PGG.

### Audit Verdicts

| Claim | Verdict | Reasoning |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| C1: word_eval = branching program | **SUSPICIOUS** | Isomorphic structure but different uses. BP is input-dependent; word_eval is fixed-word (dealer-sampled). |
| C2: Barrington used for iO | **TRUE** | GGH13 iO confirmed. |
| C3: CPS framework = PGG | **FALSE** | CPS needs per-party commuting permutations. PGG has a single global word. Structurally different. |
| C4: S5 non-solvability core to PGG | **PARTIALLY TRUE** | Used in optional Barrington demo code, not in core protocol. |
| C5: Trace equivalence relevant to BP | **FALSE** | Barrington has fixed evaluation order. Cartier-Foata reordering is orthogonal. |
| C6: Grover hardness from search space | **SUSPICIOUS** | word_eval has algebraic structure; not unstructured search. Aspirational but unsubstantiated. |
| C7: No Barrington formalization exists | **TRUE** | Confirmed across Coq, Isabelle, Lean. |
| C8: "Oblivious BP evaluation" framing | **SUSPICIOUS** | Non-standard term. Partial fit. Better termed "dealer-sampled word products." |

### Bottom Line

"The structural similarity (product of selected permutations) is real but the USES are different. Barrington selects permutations based on INPUT BITS (computation). PGG selects permutations from a RANDOM WORD (secret sharing). These are fundamentally different operations wearing similar algebra."

## Direction 2: Oblivious Permutation Evaluation / Oblivious Group Computation

### Research Findings

- Oblivious permutation IS well-developed: Keller-Scholl CRYPTO 2014, ASIACRYPT 2014, 2025 COP protocols. Definition: sender has data x, receiver has permutation pi, compute pi(x) securely.
- Oblivious Linear Group Actions exist: CCS 2021 (Attrapadung et al.) for matrix groups.
- Secret-shared shuffle well-developed: ASIACRYPT 2020 and follow-up work.
- Monodromy/covering space cryptography NOT found: direct searches yield zero results.
- PGG-SMC is at intersection of established (permutation eval, group actions) and novel (algebraic rigidity, covering scheme, monodromy). But the community has avoided building directly on topological structures.

### Audit Verdicts

| Claim | Verdict | Reasoning |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| C1: OP is well-developed primitive | **TRUE but PGG is not this** | OP = party has data + party has perm. PGG = dealer samples word, all parties get endpoints. Different problem. |
| C2: OLGA (CCS 2020) most relevant | **FALSE** | Wrong year (2021). Wrong domain (matrix/linear groups vs finite perm groups). |
| C3: Monodromy walk = mix network | **FALSE** | Single permutation evaluation, not routing pipeline through multiple nodes. |
| C4: Secret-shared shuffle related | **FALSE** | Shuffle permutes a DATABASE. PGG evaluates a single permutation at single points. Orthogonal. |
| C5: RAAG crypto proposed | **PARTIALLY TRUE** | Proposed (arXiv 1610.06495) but viability unproven. No follow-up validation. |
| C6: Braid crypto cautionary tale | **TRUE** | Confirmed broken. Does not apply to PGG (finite groups, not infinite braid groups). |
| C7: PGG at intersection of established primitives | **FALSE** | Not at intersection. In a relatively isolated space. |
| C8: "Oblivious group computation" is novel | **SUSPICIOUS** | Novel perhaps because nobody found it useful. Novelty does not equal significance. |

### Bottom Line

"PGG-SMC is NOT oblivious permutation evaluation, NOT oblivious group computation, NOT secret-shared shuffle. The established primitives solve different problems. PGG is in a dead zone."

## Direction 3: Mixing Networks / Anonymous Communication

### Research Findings

- Spectral gap analysis applies to both mixing time bounds and PGG security parameters.
- Chaum mix networks use permutation shuffling, but batch permutation of messages vs single-element evaluation.
- Three mathematical connections: (a) spectral gap bounds convergence in both; (b) fiber counting = endpoint distribution; (c) permutation group as underlying algebra.
- Honest self-assessment from research agent: "genuine but surface-level mathematical connection, not a deep protocol connection." The FFT analogy: "FFT appears in both signal processing and integer factorization. They share the mathematics, but you don't say 'signal processing is secretly an integer factoring algorithm.'"

### Audit Verdicts

| Claim | Verdict | Reasoning |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| C1: Same spectral gap math | **SUSPICIOUS** | Shared tool, different structural contexts. |
| C2: Chaum mix algebraically similar | **FALSE** | Batch permutation (mix networks) is structurally different from single-element action (PGG-SMC). |
| C3: Coq shuffle proofs exist (USENIX 2021) | **FALSE** | Claim appears fabricated; no such paper found. |
| C4: Fiber entropy contributes to anonymity | **SUSPICIOUS** | Different information-theoretic questions. Link is indirect/incidental. |
| C5: Connection is "genuine but surface-level" | **TRUE** | Accurate self-assessment. Shared math tools, different problems. |
| C6: Danezis 2003 uses expander topology | **TRUE** | Confirmed. But uses expanders as network topology, not as representation structure. |
| C7: PGG proves first spectral gap bounds | **FALSE** | PGG-SMC AXIOMATIZES spectral bounds (SchreierCertificate), does not PROVE them. Critical distinction. |
| C8: No cross-pollination possible | **TRUE** | Connection is hollow for practical purposes. |

### Bottom Line

"The connection is empty for practical purposes."

## Cross-Cutting Analysis

### What survived audit (TRUE across directions)

- Barrington IS used in modern crypto (iO, randomized encodings) -- but PGG is not a Barrington construction
- No Barrington formalization exists in any proof assistant
- Braid group crypto is broken (cautionary tale, but doesn't apply to PGG's finite groups)
- The surface-level mathematical similarities are real (shared algebra) -- but don't constitute protocol connections
- PGG-SMC axiomatizes (not proves) spectral gap bounds

### What failed audit (FALSE)

| Failed claim | Pattern |
|-------------|---------|
| CPS = PGG | **Category error**: similar algebra, different protocol structure |
| Trace equivalence relevant to BP | **Orthogonal**: Barrington has fixed order |
| Monodromy walk = mix network | **Category error**: single evaluation vs batch routing |
| Secret-shared shuffle related | **Category error**: database permutation vs point evaluation |
| PGG at intersection of established primitives | **Wishful thinking**: actually isolated |
| USENIX 2021 Coq shuffle proofs | **Fabricated citation** |
| PGG proves spectral gap bounds | **Misrepresentation**: axiomatizes, not proves |

Common failure mode: **surface-level algebraic similarity mistaken for protocol-level connection.**

### Suspicious claims requiring further investigation

- `word_eval` as "oblivious branching program evaluation" -- could be formalized as a variant, but would need new definitions that diverge from standard Barrington
- Grover hardness from search space size -- needs concrete lower bounds, not handwaving
- Fiber entropy contributing to anonymity -- would need to formulate a precise anonymity definition and prove a reduction

## What IS Genuinely Valuable

The project contains real mathematical contributions that survive audit:

1. **AlgebraicRigidity theorem** -- a proved classification of the security-threshold tradeoff parameterized by covering genus. Novel mathematical content with a clean statement.

2. **Fiber entropy analysis** -- exact closed-form entropy for pushforward distributions on group actions, with Pinsker bridge to statistical distance. Clean information theory independent of PGG.

3. **Cartier-Foata formalization** -- trace monoid counting for RAAG generators. Independent combinatorial contribution with connections to concurrency theory.

4. **The formalization itself** -- 57 files in Rocq/MathComp with 0 Admitted. Rare achievement in cryptographic formalization.

## 4 Possible Honest Framings

### Option A: Impossibility / Classification Result

> "We formalize the limits of monodromy-based secret sharing. AlgebraicRigidity classifies what's achievable as a function of genus. The answer: strictly weaker than Shamir on every axis."

**Venue fit:** ProvSec, TCC (if the impossibility is sufficiently general)
**Risk:** Reviewers may ask "why study something you proved is weak?"
**Strength:** Honest, novel classification theorem, clean negative result

### Option B: Formalization Methodology Contribution

> "We demonstrate Rocq/MathComp formalization of a novel cryptographic construction, yielding machine-checked security bounds. 57 files, 0 Admitted."

**Venue fit:** CPP, ITP, Journal of Formalized Reasoning
**Risk:** ProvSec reviewers may not value the formalization effort itself
**Strength:** The formalization IS impressive; matches Affeldt 2007 template

### Option C: Information-Theoretic Techniques (lead with fiber entropy)

> "We develop exact entropy formulas for pushforward distributions on group actions and apply them to security analysis. The formulas are general; the application to monodromy is one instantiation."

**Venue fit:** ISIT, Entropy journal, ProvSec (if framed as technique paper)
**Risk:** The "application" (PGG) may seem unmotivated without the broader story
**Strength:** The entropy results are independently useful

### Option D: Abandon ProvSec

Submit to a formalization venue (CPP, ITP) or a combinatorics venue (FPSAC) instead. The Cartier-Foata and algebraic rigidity results may fit better outside cryptography.

**Risk:** Feels like giving up on the original goal
**Strength:** Honest match of contribution to audience

---

## Recommendation

PGG-SMC cannot be honestly repositioned as an instance of any established cryptographic primitive. All three alternative framings (Barrington, oblivious permutation, mixing networks) fail under adversarial audit -- the connections are algebraic surface-level, not protocol-level.

**The honest path forward is one of Options A-D above**, not a reframing that will invite the same "but Shamir is better" criticism dressed in different clothes. Options B (formalization) and C (entropy techniques) have the strongest independent merit. Option A (impossibility) is the most intellectually honest for ProvSec specifically.

Do not attempt to claim PGG-SMC "is really" a branching program / oblivious permutation / mix network. Reviewers will see through it, and the audit results show why.
